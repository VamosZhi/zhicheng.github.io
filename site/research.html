01

Fluid-structure interaction

Efficient models for coupled dynamics, applied to aeroelasticity and structural design.

1.1

Model development

1.1.1

Structural reduced-order modeling

I project fluid loads onto dominant structural modes and reconstruct deformation through modal superposition. This reduces the structural degrees of freedom in coupled simulations of flexible bodies, including hydrofoils interacting with cavitating flows.

Int. J. Multiph. Flow, 2025

Media source

Marine research presentation, slide 11.

1.1.2

Fluid reduced-order modeling

I combine selective frequency damping with system identification to build compact fluid models. Coupling these models to structural equations enables rapid instability prediction and modal analysis, including non-uniform inflows through submodal superposition.

Phys. Fluids, 2022; J. Fluid Mech., 2024

Media source

Aerodynamics / aeroelasticity / aeroacoustics presentation, slide 13.

1.1.3

Graph neural networks

I develop mesh-adaptive graph models for unsteady flow and fluid-structure prediction. Hypergraph representations support changing meshes and moving boundaries, providing fast surrogates for oscillating, rotating, and flexible structures.

Eng. Appl. Artif. Intell., 2026

Media source

Aerodynamics / aeroelasticity / aeroacoustics presentation, slide 6.

1.2

Application studies

1.2.1

Galloping

I investigate how fore-aft tapering governs galloping onset. Simulations and wind-tunnel experiments show that a one-degree geometric change can alter instability in the studied configurations, linking cross-sectional design to fluid-to-structure energy transfer.

J. Fluid Mech., 2023; J. Fluids Struct., 2023

Media source

Marine research presentation, slide 13.

1.2.2

Transonic aeroelasticity

I study shock-boundary-layer interactions and the coupling of buffet with structural motion. In tandem-wing configurations, an upstream canard modifies the downstream wake, with distinct effects on plunge and pitch instability.

Canard-wing interaction and flutter prediction

Media source

Aerodynamics / aeroelasticity / aeroacoustics presentation, slides 16-18.

1.2.3

Topology design

I resolve internal structural architecture within high-fidelity FSI simulations. Bio-inspired topologies redistribute deformation and flow-induced energy, creating design options for lightweight structures with improved aeroelastic response.

High-fidelity modeling, additive manufacturing, and wind-tunnel testing

Media source

Peking University forum presentation, slide 23; topology study on slide 18.

1.2.4

Active morphing

My research direction is to couple rapid FSI prediction with controlled changes in wing shape. The aim is to adapt deformation and kinematics to operating conditions while balancing aerodynamic performance and aeroelastic stability.

02

Flow-induced noise

Predictive methods for sound generation and propagation, applied to rotating and marine systems.

2.1

Model development

2.1.1

CAPE

I develop cavitation acoustic perturbation equations to describe sound generation and propagation in two-phase flows. The formulation accounts for volumetric sources associated with cavitation dynamics, alongside flow and structural contributions.

Cavitation acoustic perturbation equations

Media source

Marine research presentation, slide 17.

2.1.2

APE + BPM + ALM

I couple acoustic perturbation equations with the Brooks-Pope-Marcolini airfoil self-noise model and an actuator-line flow model. This framework predicts rotating-blade noise without requiring a blade-resolved flow mesh.

Renew. Energy, 2022

Media source

Aerodynamics / aeroelasticity / aeroacoustics presentation, slide 9.

2.1.3

Lagrangian mesh-free vortex-particle tracking

I track the wake using Lagrangian vortex particles and connect its hydrodynamic features to acoustic models. This concentrates computation on the structures responsible for sound, supporting efficient propeller and ducted-propulsor noise prediction.

Wake dynamics and efficient hydroacoustic prediction

Media source

Marine research presentation, slide 24.

2.2

Application studies

2.2.1

Wind turbines

I apply the ALM-BPM-APE framework to wind-turbine sound generation, propagation, and directivity. The analysis connects rotor loading and turbulent wake dynamics to the acoustic field for noise-aware turbine design.

A unified framework for aeroacoustics simulation of wind turbines

Media source

Two figures supplied from my wind-turbine aeroacoustics paper, combined for website display. Labels removed and clarity enhanced with AI image editing.

2.2.2

Propeller singing

I study how flexible-blade vibration interacts with cavitation dynamics to produce narrowband singing. Coupled fluid-structure-acoustic analysis relates the tonal response to the frequencies and phase relationships of the underlying sources.

Structural flexibility, cavitation, and acoustic coherence

Media source

2.2.3

Singing analysis & integrated hull-propeller systems

I extend source-level analysis to coupled propeller, nozzle, and vessel configurations. The work examines how geometry and operating conditions modify cavitating wakes and radiated sound, guiding integrated design for quieter propulsion.

Phys. Fluids, 2025 · Editor’s Pick

Media source

Marine research presentation, slides 21-25.

2.2.4

Digital-twin control & sea trials

Sea-trial noise measurements provide an experimental basis for evaluating marine propulsion models. Building on these tests, my planned digital-twin work will combine rapid prediction, operating data, and feedback control to manage vibration and underwater sound.

Sea-trial validation; digital-twin control is a research direction
