Research

The physics of interaction.

My research combines high-fidelity simulation, reduced-order modeling, machine learning, and experiments to understand coupled flow–structure systems and guide engineering design.

01 / MECHANISMS

Flow-induced vibration
& aeroelasticity

How do flow instabilities and structural motion reinforce one another?

I investigate vortex-induced vibration, galloping, buffet, and flutter using coupled simulations, modal analysis, and experiments. My work on fore–aft tapering shows that small changes in cross-sectional geometry can have a pronounced effect on galloping onset.

In the studied configurations, changing a cross-sectional angle by only one degree altered whether galloping was triggered. This work connects geometric design to the transfer of energy between a flow and an oscillating structure.

My aeroelasticity research also examines how an upstream canard modifies the buffet and pitching response of a downstream main wing in transonic flow.

02 / MULTIPHYSICS

Vibration &
acoustic radiation

Structural motion changes both the source of sound and the way it radiates.

I develop computational approaches for acoustic generation and propagation in unsteady flows, including vibrating structures, rotating systems, and cavitating flows. My work combines flow–structure simulations with acoustic perturbation methods.

For oscillating cylinders in turbulent flow, I studied how vibration frequency modifies vortex shedding and the resulting acoustic response. In the investigated cases, an oscillation-to-shedding frequency ratio near 0.57 produced strong noise reduction. The result identifies a route to tuning vibration and noise together, with the relevant operating conditions retained.

I also examine interference between acoustic components and the role of cavitation as a volumetric sound source.

03 / MARINE SYSTEMS

Cavitation &
hydroelastic dynamics

Understanding the interaction of flexible structures, cavitating flows, and underwater sound.

My work on flexible tapering hydrofoils investigates how structural deformation and sheet cavitation affect flow-induced vibration. The research combines high-fidelity flow simulation with structural dynamics to resolve their coupled response.

I also study propeller singing, examining how structural vibration and cavitation-related sources can combine to produce narrowband acoustic radiation.

I have also investigated Lagrangian vortex tracking as an efficient basis for predicting propeller noise. This approach follows key wake features and couples them to acoustic models, reducing the need to resolve the entire flow domain at uniform fidelity.

04 / COMPUTATIONAL METHODS

Rapid prediction
& structural design

Models that retain the dynamics needed for prediction and design.

I develop system-identification and reduced-order approaches for fast stability analysis. My submodal superposition approach addresses three-dimensional, non-uniform inflows and has achieved up to 2,000× acceleration for the non-uniform inflow configurations studied.

My machine-learning work uses graph neural networks to represent unsteady flows on changing meshes. A mesh-adaptive hypergraph model addresses flow around oscillating and rotating structures. Ongoing work extends graph-based prediction to flexible wings and coupled fluid–structure dynamics.

At Berkeley, I also study internal topology as a means of regulating flow–structure energy transfer. This work connects lightweight structural design to passive aeroelastic control.

Research vision

Integrated modeling,
inverse design & control.

My long-term goal is an integrated fluid–thermal–structure interaction framework. I plan to connect high-fidelity multiphysics models with graph-based predictors that remain useful across changing geometries, mesh resolutions, and operating conditions.

These predictors will support inverse design and digital-twin control for aerospace, energy, and marine systems. Future work will explore internal topologies that balance vibration suppression, thermal performance, weight, and manufacturability, together with experimental validation.
