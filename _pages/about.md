My research expertise mainly lies in the flow dynamics and fluid-structure interaction (FSI), with the interfacing of custom high-fidelity solvers, data-driven modelings, multiphase dynamics, and experiments. Currently, I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at UC Berkeley, with research initiatives on transonic aeroelasticity and bio-inspired flexible flapping wings. This project involves integrating physics-based machine learning with computational FSI dynamics to enhance modelling efficiency. At Berkeley, my concerns about the FSI problem extended to the internal topology and materials. During my Ph.D. at the University of Waterloo and Duke University, I focused on modelling development and the multi-scale mechanism analysis of flow-induced vibration (FIV) and its coupling with multiphysics. My work led to novel discoveries and data-driven models in FIV systems. Those works garnered significant attention for their practical utility, including interest from the NASA Aeroelasticity Group. At UBC, I joined two industrial projects cooperating with Seaspan, Clearseas, and Robert Allan Ltd., in which I expanded my expertise to hydro flow-structure interactions and machine learning.
